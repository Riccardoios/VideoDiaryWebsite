`app.md` was compiled from the US listing on **2026-07-14**. Anything marked **[verify]** below drifts and must be re-checked against the live listing before it appears in an article.

## What the app is

Video Diary is a video journaling app for iPhone. You open it, it's already on the camera, you tap record and talk. Entries land on a calendar. A montage maker stitches daily clips into one film.

The positioning in one line: **a diary, not a platform.** No AI, no feed, no account, no sharing.

### Premium prices are storefront-specific

Different storefronts sell different products. The App Store product page lists every configured IAP whether or not that storefront's paywall offers it, which is why the raw listing looks self-contradictory — a weekly that costs more than a monthly. What is actually on sale:

| Product | US | IT |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly Premium | $6.99 | not sold |
| Monthly Premium | not sold | €3.99 |
| Yearly Premium | $44.99 | €26.99 |
| Exclusive Offer | $26.99 | €20.99 |

**Never quote a price without naming the storefront, and check that the storefront actually sells that product first.** Writing "$5.99/month" for a US reader who can only buy weekly or yearly is a factual error about our own product — and it is the easy mistake to make, because a listing dump shows all four products at once. `app.md` carries the current table.

## Real strengths (the ones worth building articles around)

- **Opens on the camera.** No launch screen, no dashboard, no account wall. Time-from-open-to-recording is the best single predictor of whether a journaling habit survives, and this is the app's core bet.
- **No AI touches the entries.** No cloud transcription, no summarization, no "insights" generated by a model somewhere else. For a diary, this is a feature, and it is increasingly rare.
- **Calendar-first structure.** The month view is the difference between a journal and a camera roll full of loose selfie videos.
- **The montage payoff.** Recording is the habit; rewatching is the reward. Any clip length, not restricted to one second.
- **Mood tracking with an Insight view** for patterns over time.
- **Free for the core loop.** Premium buys extras, not the ability to record today.
- **Ships on Vision Pro and Mac**, which most competitors don't.

## Honest weaknesses (use these — they are what makes a page credible)

- **iOS only.** No Android, no web. If the reader is on Android, say so and point them elsewhere; pretending otherwise wastes their time and yours.
- **No searchable transcripts.** The no-AI stance means you cannot search what you said, only what you typed in notes. For someone who wants to find "that time I talked about the job offer", a transcribing app genuinely serves them better. Say it.
- **Video takes space.** Daily video is heavier than daily text. iCloud backup is a Premium feature.
- **No sharing or social features.** Deliberate, but if someone wants to publish their vlog, this is the wrong app.
- **Not a written-journal replacement.** For long reflective writing, a text-first app is better.

Naming these costs nothing and buys the credibility that makes the recommendation land. It also filters out downloads that would churn in a day.

## Competitors

Verify each against its current App Store listing before writing about it. Do not state prices or features from memory.

| App | Its real strength | Where Video Diary differs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Second Everyday | Pioneered the daily-clip montage; strong community | Built around the one-second constraint; Video Diary allows any clip length and is journal-first rather than highlight-reel-first |
| Day One | Excellent written journal, mature, cross-platform | Text-native with video attached; Video Diary is video-native |
| Apple Journal | Free, built in, well integrated | Text and suggestion driven, iPhone-only surface, not a video diary |
| Journey / Diarly | Cross-platform written journaling | Same text-first gap |
| AI journaling apps (varies) | Transcription, summaries, prompts from a model | They process entries in the cloud; Video Diary deliberately does not |
| Daylio | Fast mood tracking, no writing needed | Mood only, no video record |

The honest framing across all of them: these are good apps that solve adjacent problems. The gap they all leave open is a private, video-native daily diary. That framing is true, which is why it works.

## Voice

Warm, plain, unhurried. Short declarative sentences. Concrete over abstract. Opinions stated as opinions. No hype adjectives, no exclamation marks, no "revolutionary" or "seamless" or "game-changing".

Sample from the live site, for calibration:

> A camera roll full of selfie videos is not a journal. The difference is structure: entries pinned to a calendar, so you can scroll back through your months and see the shape of your year.

> This is the single best predictor of whether you'll still be journaling in three months. Every screen between launching the app and pressing record is a place your habit goes to die.

Note what those do: a concrete image, a specific claim, no adjectives doing the work. Match that.

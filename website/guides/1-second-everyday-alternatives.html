1 Second Everyday alternatives

By Riccardo Carlotto · Updated August 2026

Most people go looking for a 1 Second Everyday alternative for one of three reasons: the watermark on their exports, the ten-second ceiling on clips, or the $49.99 a year it costs to lift either. Those are real, and they are all fixable by switching. But before you do, it's worth knowing that some of the alternatives solve a slightly different problem than the one 1SE is built for — and if a one-second-a-day film is genuinely what you want, the honest answer is that 1SE is still very good at it.

Quick answer: If you want to talk to the camera each day rather than clip a moment, Video Diary is the closest alternative on iPhone — free to record, no watermark, no clip-length limit, and nothing leaves your device. If you want the same one-second mashup without a subscription, One Second: Daily Movie Diary sells its unlocks outright. On Android, One Second Diary is free and open source. If you'd rather write and attach the occasional video, use Day One or the free Apple Journal.

What 1 Second Everyday puts behind Pro

It's worth being precise about this, because most articles on the subject are vague. On the US App Store page today, 1SE lists Pro at $9.99 per month or $49.99 per year, plus Unlimited Backup at $4.49 and Remove 1SE Watermark at $4.99 as separate purchases.

What Pro covers: extended clip length up to ten seconds, more than one snippet per day, multiple projects, the licensed music library, cloud backup, collaboration and brightness editing. What stays free: the daily capture itself, smart-fill from your camera roll, unlimited mashing into a finished video, notes and reminders.

So the free tier is not crippled — you can genuinely keep a one-second-a-day project for nothing. The pinch comes at the two moments people care about most: when you want to say more than a second's worth, and when you want to share the film without a logo on it.

Where 1 Second Everyday still beats all of these

This is the part I'd want to read if I were you, given that I make one of the alternatives.

1SE is the most polished app in this category by some distance. It holds 4.8 stars from roughly 48,000 US ratings, it was updated the day before I wrote this, and it has a decade of refinement in the one thing it does: turning a year of snippets into a film that's genuinely pleasant to watch. Smart-fill, which populates a missed day from clips already in your camera roll, is the single most effective anti-guilt feature anyone in this category has shipped. It runs on Android as well as iPhone. And it has the music library, which matters more to the finished film than people expect.

If your goal is a highlight reel — a year compressed into four minutes, set to music, watchable by other people — nothing below is better. Switch for price, privacy, or clip length. Don't switch expecting a better montage.

The six alternatives

Video Diary — for talking, not clipping

Best for: someone who wants to say how the day went, at whatever length that takes, and have nobody listen back.

This is mine, so read the criticism and discount the praise. It opens directly on the camera — one tap from home screen to recording, no account, no onboarding, no feed. Entries land on a calendar with an optional mood and a written note, and the montage maker stitches them into one film. There's no cap on entry length and no watermark on what comes out. Nothing is transcribed, summarized or sent to a model; entries stay on your device or your own iCloud.

Recording is free, permanently. Premium ($6.99/week or $44.99/year on the US store) adds colour palettes, fonts, Face ID lock and iCloud backup. It runs on iPhone, Apple silicon Macs and Vision Pro, and holds 4.5 stars from 30 ratings — a small enough base that you should weigh it accordingly rather than treat it as proof.

Where it loses: no Android, no web, no iPad app. No smart-fill and no music library, so the montage is plainer than 1SE's. And because nothing is transcribed, you can search your written notes but not your spoken words.

One Second: Daily Movie Diary — the same format, bought outright

Best for: someone who likes 1SE's approach and objects specifically to renting it.

Grassapper's app is the closest structural match to 1SE on the App Store: daily snippets, mashed into a movie, with date and location overlays. It holds 4.5 stars from about 3,600 ratings and runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple silicon Macs and Vision.

The reason to pick it is the pricing shape. Instead of one subscription, it sells the pieces: extended clip options $1.99, watermark and last slide removal $1.99, music $2.99, remove ads $1.99, 4K $0.99 — or Unlock Everything at $34.99 once. If the parts you want are the clip length and the watermark, that's about four dollars against 1SE's $49.99 a year, and it never renews.

Where it loses: the free tier is ad-supported and holds you to a strict one second. It's less polished than 1SE, and the long IAP menu is its own kind of friction.

Leap Second — the most generous free tier

Best for: trying the format seriously before paying anyone.

Leap Second gives you a calendar, multiple videos per day, Live Photo support, monthly recaps and a "second extender" up to five seconds, with Pro at $3.99/month or $29.99/year — the cheapest paid tier here. It holds 4.6 stars from around 5,600 ratings.

Where it loses: two things you should weigh. Its last App Store update was August 2025, a year ago, which for an app holding your daily footage is a legitimate concern. And its privacy label declares that it collects location, contact info and user content, and tracks identifiers and usage data across other apps — a very different posture from the rest of this list. If that matters to you, read what to look for in a private video diary before installing it.

One Second Diary — the Android answer

Best for: Android users who want the format with nothing attached.

An open-source app (MIT licensed) by the developer KyleKun: record one to ten seconds a day, view it on a calendar, add subtitles and geotags, compile a 1080p movie whenever you want. Free, no ads, no in-app purchases, no data collection, eight languages.

Where it loses: Android only, so it's not an option for anyone reading this on an iPhone. It's a volunteer project, which means fewer features and no support desk. But for the specific person who wants the 1SE concept without a subscription or a privacy policy to read, it's hard to argue with.

Day One — if you were always going to write

Best for: people whose entries are mostly words with the occasional clip.

Day One is the best written journal on any platform, with 4.8 stars from about 118,000 ratings, end-to-end encryption, and apps on iOS, Android, Mac and the web. It holds photos, video and audio, so it can absorb a video habit — it just isn't built around one.

Pricing on the US store: Silver at $8.99/month or $49.99/year, which is the tier that unlocks unlimited media, and Gold at $74.99, which adds AI journaling — daily chat, entry summaries, smart titles. That last part is a feature or a dealbreaker depending on how you feel about a model reading your diary.

Where it loses: a daily video habit in Day One means more taps than any dedicated app here, and there's no montage at the end of the year.

Apple Journal — free, and already installed

Best for: spending nothing and starting tonight.

Apple's own Journal app is free forever, on your phone already, and holds 4.8 stars from over 300,000 ratings. It suggests entries based on your photos, workouts and music, which is a genuinely good prompt engine, and it locks with Face ID.

Where it loses: it's text-first with media attached, iPhone-only, and there's no way to turn a year of entries into a film. As a video diary it's a workaround, not a solution.

The decision that actually matters

Every comparison of these apps turns into a price table, and price is the least interesting difference. The real fork is what you want to exist at the end of the year.

1SE and its clones are built to produce a film. The one-second constraint isn't a limitation they'd remove if they could — it's the whole design. One second is short enough that no day is too dull to record, and a year of them compresses into something watchable. The entry is raw material; the mashup is the product.

A video diary is built to produce entries. You talk for forty seconds about the argument you had, or the thing your kid said, and that recording is the point. It has to be long enough to hold a thought, private enough that you don't perform, and structured enough that you can find March again. The film at the end is a bonus, not the goal.

Those two things feel similar for the first week and diverge completely by month three. If you have tried 1SE and quietly felt that one second wasn't enough to say anything, you don't want a cheaper 1SE — you want a different kind of daily entry. And if you've enjoyed the discipline of the tiny clip, don't let a subscription price talk you into an app that removes the constraint you were benefitting from; the one-second habit works precisely because it asks so little.

Which one should you switch to

  • You want to say more than a second and keep it private: Video Diary.
  • You want 1SE's format but not a subscription: One Second: Daily Movie Diary, unlocked outright.
  • You want to spend nothing and don't mind ads: Leap Second — with the privacy label read first.
  • You're on Android: One Second Diary, or stay on 1SE, which has an Android version.
  • You mostly write: Day One, or Apple Journal if free matters more than features.
  • You want the best year-in-four-minutes film: stay with 1SE and pay for Pro. It's the best at that.

If it's the one-second limit that sent you here

Video Diary opens on the camera and lets you talk for as long as the thought takes. No account, no feed, no AI, no watermark. Entries land on a calendar with a mood and a note, and the montage maker turns them into one film whenever you want to watch the year back.

Free for daily journaling, on iPhone, Mac and Vision Pro. Try Video Diary free — and if what you actually wanted was the highlight reel, keep 1SE. I'd rather you kept journaling than switched.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free alternative to 1 Second Everyday?

Yes. Video Diary is free to record every day on iPhone, with no watermark and no cap on clip length; its Premium tier only adds palettes, fonts, Face ID lock and iCloud backup. On Android, One Second Diary is free, open source, ad-free and collects no data. Apple Journal is free too, but it is text-first rather than video-first.

How much does 1 Second Everyday cost in 2026?

The app is free to download. On the US App Store today, 1SE Pro is listed at $9.99 per month or $49.99 per year, and the page also lists Unlimited Backup at $4.49 and Remove 1SE Watermark at $4.99 as separate purchases. Pro covers extended clip length, multiple snippets per day, the music library and cloud backup.

Can I move my 1 Second Everyday clips to another app?

There is no direct transfer between these apps. Export your finished mashup from 1SE and save the individual clips to your camera roll first, then keep that file as your archive of everything up to now and start the new app from today. Trying to backfill a year of entries is the fastest way to abandon the habit again.

Do I have to record exactly one second?

Only in apps built around that constraint. 1 Second Everyday extends clips to ten seconds on Pro, and One Second Diary allows one to ten seconds for free. Video Diary places no limit on entry length, which suits talking to the camera rather than clipping a moment. Pick the constraint that matches what you want to end up with.
